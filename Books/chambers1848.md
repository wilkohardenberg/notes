Robert Chambers (1848, 5) claimed that the uniformity in sea level changes registered by geologists in Great Britain, Ireland, and France suggests that it was caused by a "recession of the sea" rather than an eleveation of the land. Indeed, he states, it is hard to believe that the crust of the earth could move over such a great part of its crust by maintaining "the original relation of the levels of its various parts to the centre of the earth." "Between Paris and Inverness not a vertical foot of derangement could be detected." Nonetheless, not to appear dogmatic he prefers to continue throughout his text of relative changes rather than of one or the other cause.
