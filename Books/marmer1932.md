p. 233 "Instead of a float rising and falling with the tide, the varying pressure of the water on the sea bottom may be used to indicate the varying height of water. Gauges of this type are known as pressure gauges. Along the coas, where fixed supports for installing float gauges may be found, pressure gauges are used only for special purposes. Away from the coast, however, the pressure gauge furnishes the only practical means, at the present time, for automatically  recording the rise and fall of the tide. The form devised by the French hdrographer Louis Favé, called by him the "Marégraphe plongeur," has been used in depths up to 100 fathoms and appears serviceable in even greater depths."
