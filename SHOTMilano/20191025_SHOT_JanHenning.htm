Now, in Everyday Technologies #shotmilano, Jan Henning (U Toronto) on history of fire alarm telegraph in late 19th c. Germany -- Risk response is a social construct: the consequent improvement of life does not affect all, but is class-dependent and has a clear political dimension
primary interest of late 19th c. fire rescue activities was the safety and protection of property: books, furniture, etc. Seems already a clear sign to me of who was most interested, supported, and directed the whole activity #shotmilano
Telegraph fire alarm boxes however were evenly distributed through the city, without excluding areas by class, as happened for other utilities. At the beginning however, keys to the boxes were in the hands of select persons, acting as social filters to misuse #shotmilano
Distrust to the public was the main reason for the British in delaying implementation a similar system in their cities #shotmilano
at least 50% of members of municipal council in Frankfurt had to be house owners at the time. This affected the way municipal public services, such as fire safety, were offered, activated and managed
Jan Henning wants us to bring home at least one thought from his #shotmilano talk on fire alarm telegraphs: the improvement and technologisation of safety procedures often leads to an increase of surveillance and a reduction of freedom
For more details on Jan Henning's talk have a look also at @DollyJorgensen's thread
https://twitter.com/DollyJorgensen/status/1187660515042086912?s=20
