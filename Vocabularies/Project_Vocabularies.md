#### Vocabularies of Change

The project looks at the ways in which environmental change has been described and apprehended. Disciplinarily the project addresses different aspects, that can all be enclosed in the boundary region between environmental history, history of science and history of technology. At its roots lies the desire to clarify the ways in which humans (have) face(d) changes in the natural world surrounding them. In doing so it aims at producing new perspectives on how "change" in human history can be framed and how these can affect ideas of periodisation in different disciplinary settings.

Science as it is nowadays intended, and human civilization in general. developed in a time of relative environmental and climate stability. The Holocene has made us what we are and radically influenced our view of how the world functions. Discourse about the Anthropocene, climate change and no-analog futures have in recent years made it clear how this might have been just an impression, that has however durabilly influenced how we act and imposed some serious path-dependencies. Similar paths to the idea that this may only be an impression have arisen from researches in "deep time".

##### Working groups/research interests:  

- **Past Changes** (PC): baselines (#histsci/#envhist)
  - What are the epistemic tools we use to make sense of change and how do we think/have we thought about changes that occurred in the past?
    - industrialisation
    - little ace age
  - "golden spike"
  
- **Current Changes** (CC): acceptance of change / reaction to ongoing changg (#envhist/#polhist)
  - ​	How do human communities cope with environmental change while it happens?
    - subsidence/coastal erosion
    - rights/conflict/conservation
    - desertification
- **Future Changes** (FC): prediction/modelling (#histsci/#histtech)
  - How are future changes predicted/tracked, acted upon
    - disasters
    - trends
    - models/simulations

The cases will be all considered historically, the terminology notwithstanding: the project will look at a plurality of past futures and future pasts.
