**Assessment of teaching qualifications**

I had the opportunity to get acquainted with Dr. Hardenberg's teaching skills in my capacity as deputy director of the Hermann von Helmholtz-Zentrum für Kulturtechnik, the centre responsible for organizing the interdisciplinary study programme "Diversity of Knowledge" and selecting its guest professors. In the summer semester of 2018 Dr. Hardenberg has taught, as part of this programme, a lecture, three seminars and a workshop, all thematically focused on the sea. 

Each course looked at different aspects of the history of modern oceanography and of the perception of the marine world, all interconnected by an overarching attention for the sea as an epistemic object:

- Das Meer in der Moderne. Zur Wissens- und Umweltgeschichte eines globalen Raumes [The modern sea. For a knowledge and environmental history of a global space], lecture
- Zwischen Land und Meer. Küsten, Messungen, Darstellungen [Between land and sea. Coasts, measurements, representations], seminar
- Lebenswelten. Meeresbiologie im Anthropozän [Lifeworlds. Marine biology in the Anthropocene], seminar
- Die Tiefsee in Wissenschaft und Fiktion [The deep sea in science and fiction], seminar
- Ausstellung der sieben Meere [Exhibiting the seven seas], workshop

The lecture course focused on the sea as on object of analytical knowledge and as a space of interaction between environment and society. Dr. Hardenberg has impressively achieved to offer students with extremely varied academic backgrounds the necessary overview of the history of modern oceanography. Furthermore, the intrinsic interdisciplinarity of marine environmental history has given students the possibility not only to connect current debates on the preservation and sustainable management of the oceans with the historical dimension, but also to combine them with their own prior knowledge. Current news constantly informed the lectures and were framed in historical context. To guarantee the continuity of the course through individual lectures Dr. Hardenberg introduced each session with a recap of the previous one and left always room at the end for a thorough group discussion of the discussed topics.

In the seminars Dr. Hardenberg was able, through the implementation of interdisciplinary discussions, to lead the classes into the exploration of different aspects of the historical perception of the sea as a scientific, cultural, and political object. This has produced very constructive, productive, and lively debates, in which the students were able to analyse and discuss, beside the specific topic of the session, their prior knowledge and epistemological beliefs.

Thanks to his clever selection of guests in the natural sciences and humanities, as well as the embedding of visits to the Berlin Aquarium, the Natural History Museum, and the German Museum of Technology, Dr. Hardenberg offered the students also a look behind the scenes of current research. The students also perceived the suggested literary and media studies readings as enormous enrichments of the seminars, as they greatly contributed to provide a broadly interdisciplinary understanding of the discussion topics.

The very heterogeneous student body showed great interest for the themes approached in the lectures and discussions. This was also the case when the topics were apparently very distant from their own studies. The teaching concept, based on the idea of analysing complex systems of relationship through the focus on a concrete knowledge object, stimulated the students' interest and curiosity.

In the workshop Dr. Hardenberg cooperated with the Archives of Humboldt University to give students an opportunity to work on primary sources on the history of the former Berlin Museum of Oceanography. The aim was to use the history of that museum to produce ideas and proposals for a modern transdisciplinary museum of the sea. The group developed jointly a plan for a virtual exhibition.

During his guest professorship Dr. Hardenberg supervised the final thesis of a master student. This is an extremely rare occurrence within the framework of the optional "Diversity of Knowledge" study programme. Thanks to his personal, highly committed and effective teaching style Dr Hardenberg was always able to connect the students' enthusiasm for the topic with the necessary coherence in terms of content.
