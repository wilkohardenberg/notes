Some of the most famous poachers had been hired as game wardens by the king, in an attempt to make good use of their exceptional knowledge of the landscape and of the ibex' behavior. , This had beem the case of a certain Gabriel Berard from Aymavilles, famous for his hunting skills and for his commerce in live ibex cubs, which he sold in Switzerland and Austria. According to Brocherel (1908, 447) King Vittorio Emanuele II went to see him in person at home to offer him a post in his game wardens corps (Tibaldi 1904, 50-2).

Felice Ferrero gives the responsibility for the ever reduced distribution area of the ibex to the advent of tourism in the fifty years preceding the publication of his book on the Valle d'Aosta in 1910 (Ferrero 1910, 75)

Monti (1913) wrote an extensive article discussing the border of forested areas in the Gran Paradiso.

The destructive impact of the local population on the ibex, in conjunction with the greedy desire of foreigners for horns and furs, has been repeatedly mentioned as a reason to set up punitive laws to preserve them. Only the intervention of the king made it possible for the species not to go extinct (1914 "La fauna").

Parpagliolo (1918, 154), in an article supporting the plans for the institution of a national park in the Val di Sangro in Abruzzo, remembers of the predecessors of Sarasin's idea of the reconstitution of a natural biocenosis in the Swiss National Park: the Theresienhain by Bamberg, the Hasbruch forest in Oldenburg with its millenial oaks, the island Wilm by Rügen, in which vegetation is left by its owner to its own, the Kubany forest in Bohemia. All these, states Parpagliolo, are examples of the long-standing need to preserve to some extent the freedom of the forces of nature.

The royal commission offered, as part of a broad programme for the protection of alpine plants,  prizes aimed at regenerating rare plants such as edelweiss, genepy and other officinal plants (Brizi 1928, 121)

Pompeo Viglino, a solicitor in Turin, addressed a letter in 1919 to the magazine of the Italian Touring Club supporting the need to institute a park in the former hunting reserve (1919, "Per un parco nazionale"). The TCI supported the initiative and stressed the urgency of the deed, since every passing year may prove fatal to the last relics of the ibex.
