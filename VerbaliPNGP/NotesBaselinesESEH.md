In 1913 Pampanini, in an early text about the need to create national parks thorughout the Alps to contribute to the reintroduction of the ibex, stresses how these should only be set up in the regions from which the ibex had disappeared recently, as not to alter the existing balance of flora and fauna. National parks, by assuring the ibex its natural life conditions through the removal of all disruptive human activities, would have offered the better potential for their preservation (Pampanini 1913, 46).
