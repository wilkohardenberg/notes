
Relation between macro and micro dynamics

Earth observation by remote sensing satellites collected vast arrays of local measurements into new data fabrics.

Session II: Practices
Thursday, February 18, 2021, 2pm

Questions:
AO: Are social practices a viable tool to incorporate ordinary people and underrepresented actors into the global historiography of science and technology?
AO: Practices are crucial to build specialized skills and techniques. Are they relevant concepts to study pre-indsutrial and non-Western knowledge and technological traditions?
AO: Can we connect tacit knowledge and embodied knowledge with social practices to come up with a more comprehensive understanding of knowledge production?
JS: How can we conceive the relation between the micro level of individual actions and the macro level of global structures in history writing (on science and technology) (cf. Epple, Ghobrial)?
JS: Does practice theory deliver the adequate analytical toolkit for doing so (Epple)?
JS: Or should we attempt to overcome the binaries of ‘the micro’ and ‘the macro’ — local/global and agency/structure (cf. de Vito)? 
WH: Research practices travel globally, but need often to be adapted to local social and environmental conditions. How does this tension affect our understanding of when a certain practice becomes something completely different?
WH: And how does the accumulation of knowledge about specific locales produce an understanding of global mechanics?
WH: Switching to an environmental perspective, how do local perceptions of variation relate to global understandings of change? What are the epistemic tools we use to make sense of change?
[Needs tighter link to knowledge/tech. Move from global history to practices not obvious]
