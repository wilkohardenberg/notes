Q: How does looking at the material practices of research affect our understanding of how science/technology live and act at the same time locally and globally?

- Research practices travel globally, but need often to be adapted to local social and environmental conditions. How does this tension affect our understanding of when a certain practice becomes something completely different?
- And how does the accumulation of knowledge about specific locales produce an understanding of global mechanics?
- Switching to an environmental perspective, how do local perceptions of variation relate to global understandings of change? What are the epistemic tools we use to make sense of change?
