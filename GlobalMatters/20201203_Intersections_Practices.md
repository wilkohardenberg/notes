Our first idea was to try to adopt the concept outlined by Epple and de Vito as an umbrella for the further thinking. The difficulty here appeared to be that we incurred into the risk to force the discussion within the disciplinary boundaries of practice theory. we want instead to keep the discussion open and introduce the authors and the readings without pushing the participants into a specific direction. 

As a way to escape this trap we discussed the possibility to reframe practice in a less theoretical way as — just — "the way we do things" and look at their role in bridging the local global divide. 

Furthermore, I suggested to keep the suggested lit relatively recent: participants can bring classics into the debate on their own. We should offer a collective view into recent debates and developments, beyond strict disciplinary boundaries.

A first proposal for our main question would be, in line with these thoughts

- Can attention to the history of practices help to overcome the micro/macro and local/global divides?

Below this we would offer three further questions/leads, respectively focussed on tech/sci/env. These should act as feeders for the discussion, without a need in any way to touch the all during the discussion or to look at them in any specific order.

After a broad discussion on the colonialist nature of some of the takes developed in certain of the questions we collected, the sci question has tentatively been reframed as follows:

- Local practices become global and vice versa. In both instances there is the need to adapt them to different conditions. How does this tension affect our understanding of when a certain practice becomes something completely different?

The idea is to offer neither only a bottom up nor a top down perspective, but, in line with the idea to overcome the micro/macro divide, look also at ways in which the two productively interact.

The question on env still needs further work to be polished.

- Switching to an environmental perspective, how do local perceptions of variation relate to global understandings of change? What are the epistemic tools we use to make sense of change?

And the tech questions need to be rewritten as well. Among the references mentioned taht could be of interest here there are Jacobs's *Birders of Africa* and Höhler's work on El Niño.
