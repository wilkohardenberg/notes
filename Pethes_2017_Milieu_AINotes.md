>  please provide a summation of the main points of Pethes' article

1. The concept of 'milieu' gained significant importance in 19th-century discourse across multiple fields, including life sciences, social sciences, and aesthetic/literary theory.
2. Key Historical Development:
- Originally derived from Newton's concept of "ambient medium"
- Developed through Lamarck's biological theories: "miliex environnants"
- Further refined by thinkers like Schelling, Hegel, and Claude Bernard

3. Three Major Theoretical Approaches:
- The mechanistic view (environment determining organism)
  - Views the environment as directly determining the organism
  - Assumes a one-way causal relationship from environment to organism
  - Treats the milieu as an external force that shapes and controls living things
  - Associated with early deterministic theories
  - Views organisms as passive recipients of environmental influences
  - Often criticized for being too reductionist and failing to account for organisms' adaptive capabilities
- The dialectical view (interaction between organism and environment)
  - Emphasizes the interactive relationship between organism and environment
  - Sees the relationship as bi-directional and mutually influencing
  - Recognizes that organisms can modify their environments
  - Based on the understanding that living things actively respond to environmental conditions
  - Acknowledges both environmental influence and organismic agency
  - Influenced by German Idealist philosophy, particularly Schelling and Hegel
  - Views the organism-environment relationship as dynamic and reciprocal
  - Recognizes that organisms can choose their responses to environmental conditions
- The creative/generative view (organisms creating their own environments)
  - Most sophisticated and complex of the three approaches
  - Associated particularly with Claude Bernard's concept of "milieu intérieur"
  - Argues that organisms create their own internal environments
  - Emphasizes the organism's ability to maintain internal stability
  - Suggests that living things generate the conditions for their own existence
  - Views organisms as active creators of their environmental conditions
  - Recognizes that complex organisms can increasingly isolate themselves from external environmental influences
  - Influenced later concepts like homeostasis and autopoiesis
  - Suggests that organisms become more independent of external conditions as they become more complex
  - Points toward modern understanding of biological self-organization

4. Claude Bernard's Important Contribution:
- Introduced the concept of "milieu intérieur" (internal environment)
- Argued that organisms create their own internal conditions
- Bridged the gap between mechanistic and vitalistic interpretations

5. Literary Applications:
- Influenced naturalist writers like Balzac and Zola
- Connected to Hegel's concept of "prose" as representing everyday reality
  - Prose as decline of poetry, but at the same time important exploratory moment
- Helped develop theories of literary realism and naturalism

6. Epistemological Significance:
- Challenged traditional dualistic worldviews
- Introduced the idea of self-generating environments
- Influenced later systems theory (particularly Luhmann's work)

7. Modern Relevance:
- Influenced development of systems theory
- Contributed to understanding of biological and social self-organization
- Impacted methods of scientific observation and literary representation

The article argues that the concept of 'milieu' was crucial in developing modern understandings of the relationship between organisms and their environments, and between art and reality. It remains relevant to contemporary discussions in both science and aesthetics.

The significance lies in how it helped bridge various dichotomies (internal/external, organism/environment, art/reality) and established a more dynamic understanding of these relationships, influencing both scientific and artistic thought.

- Language itself carries anthropocentric assumptions

  * Geological time scales
  * Multi-species temporalities

  * Slow processes that exceed human perception
  * Simultaneous events at different scales

- Struggles to represent distributed or collective agency
- Difficulty capturing non-human modes of experience
- Challenge of representing non-cognitive awareness

4. Scale Issues:
- Prose narrative tends to work best at human scale
- Difficulty representing:
  * Microscopic processes
  * Planetary systems
  * Complex networks
  * Distributed phenomena
  * Multiple scales simultaneously

- Human language itself carries anthropocentric assumptions
