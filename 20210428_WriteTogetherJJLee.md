The river flows from north to south. It's almost dry. Not much more than a stream. But the river bed is much wider, and unruly. There are actually more pebbles than water, stretching far into the land. At irregular intervals the river is crossed by bridges: railway bridges, road bridges, and water bridges. Yes, a canal cross the river. The water that seems to lack in the river itself flows abundantly in the major canal that brings water from west to east to irrigate the many rice fields. The whole landscape indeed, on any day in May, is saturated with water, neatly separated into individual fields by small dykes. The opposition between the almost wild river and the neatly organised chessboard of the fields couldn't be more stern. But life and unruliness abunds in the ordered, perfectly levelled fields as well. A heron rises to fly from one to the other. Frogs chant along the dykes. Water snakes slither into the water filled fields. Carps swim in the low water. A vole digs into the side of an embankment. The roots of a row of willows keeps the dyke together. Cement does the same at another corner. Or in another time. A minimal waterfall forms through a levee on the margin of two fields, allowing a constant flow that keeps the water running and fresh against an appearance of stasis. A cloud of mosquitoes hovers in the air. The air hums in the sun, the water reflects the sky and the distant Alps. 



People – till, level, breach, create

Mosquitoes – sting, infect, prevent human access

Frogs – sing, croak, feed, control the mosquito population

Voles – dig, reframe, breach

Herons – keep the frog and carp populations in check

Canals – water, flow

Carps – weed, feed

Dykes – shape, contain

Cement – keep the dykes together

Willows – consolidate, keep the dykes together

The welly-clad boots slop into the mud. The suction of water pulls the foot back into the field. Carps swim quite too close while they intently weed the field. Frogs croak excitedly. Grasping the root of one of the willows keeping the dyke together I pull myself up onto the small patch of grass. A few steps weaving among the willows I finally get my feet on solid ground. I can enjoy a moment of respite on the margin of a cement lock. A minimal fall gurgles below me keeping the water constantly running between the fields contained by the levees. My arrival disturbs a heron, intent on feeding on the frogs and fish a few metres from the lock. Majestically it rises and flies away to the adjoining field to continue its meal. Less distraught seem to be the mosquitoes, who envelop me like a cloud while I balance myself on top of the low earthen dyke to get back to the dirt road two fields down. A tractor with huge spiky wheels moves in the distance along the road, raising dirt at its passage.
