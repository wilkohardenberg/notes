Summary of the main points from "Where Humans and the Planetary Conflate—An Introduction to Environing Media" by Adam Wickberg and Johan Gärdebo:

Key Arguments:

1. Concept of Environing Media:
- Combines the ideas of "environing" (shaping environment) and "media" (technologies of mediation)
- Shows how humans shape and understand their environment through various technologies
- Emphasizes both material and conceptual aspects of environmental change

2. Historical Development:
- Major shift in human-Earth relations occurred after 1500
- Early examples include navigation and mapping technologies
- Development of scale-changing technologies like satellites
- Evolution from local to global to planetary understanding

3. Key Examples:
- Spanish colonial mapping and navigation in the Philippines
- Development of satellite technologies
- Earth system science and monitoring
- Contemporary AI and environmental data collection

4. Three Critical Aspects:
- Scale: Technologies enable understanding across different scales
- Mediation: How environmental knowledge is produced and circulated
- Integration: Combining material and conceptual changes

5. Contemporary Implications:
- Role of tech companies in environmental data collection
- Issues of data ownership and governance
- Need for humanistic engagement with environmental sciences
- Importance of historical perspective on current challenges

6. Theoretical Framework:
- Builds on concepts from environmental history
- Incorporates media theory
- Connects to discussions of the Anthropocene
- Emphasizes the role of technology in environmental understanding

7. Call to Action:
- Urges humanists to engage with environmental sciences
- Suggests need for integrated approaches
- Emphasizes importance of historical perspective
- Calls for critical analysis of environmental technologies

The authors argue that understanding environing media is crucial for:
- Understanding historical environmental change
- Addressing current environmental challenges
- Developing future environmental policies
- Bridging humanities and sciences
